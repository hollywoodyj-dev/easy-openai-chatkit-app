# 0. Executive Decision

The next stage of Wisewave search and knowledge development is formally renamed:

> **Wisewave Semantic Authority Program**  
> **Phase 2 — Reflection AI Knowledge System**

Do not treat this work as conventional "SEO Phase 2."

Phase 2 is not primarily a traffic-acquisition program.

It is not a content-volume program.

It is not an instruction to create more landing pages.

It is not an authorization to generate a large article library.

Its purpose is to establish Wisewave as an increasingly coherent, trustworthy, and referenceable semantic authority for the category:

> **Reflection AI**

The long-term target is not merely:

> Google ranks Wisewave for a keyword.

The stronger target is:

> Search engines, AI systems, external writers, and users increasingly understand Wisewave as a defining source for what Reflection AI means.

# 12. Reflection Writing Constitution

Every Phase 2 article must pass four mandatory gates.

### Gate 1 — One Question

Every article should answer one meaningful human question.

Not:

> ten tips about reflection.

Prefer:

> Why do we explain ourselves too quickly?

The article may explore complexity but must maintain one central inquiry.

### Gate 2 — One Distinction

Every article should help the reader see at least one distinction more clearly.

Examples:

```text
Event ≠ Interpretation
Feeling ≠ Identity
Reflection ≠ Rumination
Understanding ≠ Explaining
Support ≠ Advice
Uncertainty ≠ Failure
```

The distinction should support seeing.

It must not become a simplistic binary.

### Gate 3 — One Original Phrase

Every article should contain at least one sentence expressing an original Wisewave-quality idea.

The purpose is not slogan generation.

The sentence should emerge naturally from the article.

Example posture:

> Reflection does not always begin when an answer appears. Sometimes it begins when explanation stops.

Original phrases should be:

- quotable
- conceptually meaningful
- non-promotional
- compatible with Wisewave's voice

### Gate 4 — One Open Ending

Every article should preserve reflective space at the end.

The article should not close by telling the reader what to think or do.

Avoid:

> Therefore, you should...

Prefer an ending that allows the reader's own reflection to continue.
