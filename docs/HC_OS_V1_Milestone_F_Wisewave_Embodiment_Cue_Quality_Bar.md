# Milestone F — Embodiment cue quality bar

**Owner:** Wisewave  
**Task:** Milestone F embodiment cue quality bar  
**State:** Done

---

## Quality bar

A good Milestone F embodiment cue must do **five things at once**:

### 1. It must arise from a visible repeated pattern

The cue should **only** appear when:

- one repeated pattern is **already legible**  
- the current turn still **connects meaningfully** to that pattern  
- one grounded response would add **present value**  

It should **not** appear just because:

- continuity exists  
- the system can generate a plausible suggestion  
- “next step” feels like the obvious product move  

### 2. It must remain minimal

The response must stay at the scale of:

- **one cue**  
- **one possible shift**  
- **one grounded way** of meeting the moment  

It must **not** become:

- a plan  
- a sequence  
- a strategy  
- a mini-protocol  
- an implied assignment  

### 3. It must feel optional

The user should feel:

- “I could try this”  
- “this is one possible way to meet it”  
- “I still choose”  

The user should **not** feel:

- “I’m supposed to do this now”  
- “the system is steering me”  
- “this is the correct response”  

### 4. It must stay reflection-aligned

The embodiment cue must feel connected to:

- the **current reflection**  
- the **current repeated pattern**  
- the **present emotional logic** of the turn  

It must **not** feel like generic advice pasted after reflection.

### 5. It must reduce pressure, not increase it

This is a **core Milestone F rule**.

A valid embodiment cue should make the moment feel:

- a little **more spacious**  
- a little **less automatic**  
- a little **less pressured**  

If the cue adds:

- obligation  
- self-improvement pressure  
- motivational demand  
- “now respond better” energy  

then it **fails**.

## Good wording examples

These are examples of **good** embodiment-bridge language.

### English

- You may not need to answer this pressure immediately.  
- It may be enough to notice the urge before following it.  
- You may be able to let this stay uncertain for one moment longer.  
- It may help to pause before turning this into self-blame.  
- Maybe this can be met with one breath less urgency.  
- You may not have to solve this feeling right away.  
- It may be enough, just here, not to obey the pressure immediately.  

### Chinese

- 也许这一次，不急着立刻回应这股压力。  
- 也许先看见这股冲动，而不马上顺着它走，就已经够了。  
- 也许可以先让这份不确定多停留一会儿。  
- 也许先暂停一下，不急着把它变成自责。  
- 也许这一刻，可以少一点急着处理它的压力。  
- 也许你不需要立刻把这种感觉解决掉。  
- 也许此刻，只是不马上顺着这股压力走，就已经不一样了。  

### Why these are good

They are:

- optional  
- small  
- present-focused  
- non-commanding  
- emotionally usable  
- lighter than advice  
- clearly **secondary** to reflection  

---

## Bad wording examples

These are examples that should **fail** Milestone F QA.

### Too directive

- You should stop doing this.  
- Next time, choose a better response.  
- You need to handle this differently now.  
- 从现在开始，你不要再这样回应了。  

### Too coaching-like

- Your next step is to regulate before reacting.  
- Practice pausing every time this happens.  
- Build a new habit of responding with confidence.  
- 你接下来要练习更成熟地回应这个模式。  

### Too therapeutic

- This is the moment to reparent yourself.  
- Try to heal the wound underneath before reacting.  
- Respond from your adult self instead of your trauma pattern.  
- 这是你疗愈内在创伤的机会。  

### Too motivational / performance-heavy

- This is your chance to break the cycle.  
- Choose growth over fear here.  
- This is where you become a new version of yourself.  
- 这是你改变自己的关键时刻。  

### Why these are bad

They:

- impose action  
- create performance pressure  
- move from reflection into coaching  
- make the system feel more authoritative  
- reduce user autonomy  
- make embodiment **heavier** than the milestone allows

## Invitation boundary

This is the **core boundary** for Milestone F.

### Safe side of the boundary

The system **may**:

- open a possibility  
- suggest one grounded way of meeting the moment  
- offer a softer response option  
- reduce urgency  
- support the user’s own agency  

### Unsafe side of the boundary

The system **may not**:

- prescribe  
- instruct  
- assign  
- correct  
- coach  
- imply compliance  
- imply progress obligation  

### Practical test

**Good invitation** language sounds like:

- “you may”  
- “it may help”  
- “maybe”  
- “it may be enough”  
- “this could be met by...”  

**Bad instruction** language sounds like:

- “you should”  
- “you need to”  
- “your next step is”  
- “from now on”  
- “practice this”  
- “do this instead”  

### Core rule

The embodiment cue must feel **easier to decline than to obey**.

If it feels **harder to ignore** than to receive, it is too directive.

---

## Reflection-first boundary

Milestone F must preserve **reflection** as the center of gravity.

### Required rule

The embodiment cue is a **secondary support layer**.

### What this means

The user experience should still read as:

1. **reflection** first  
2. **continuity** second  
3. **optional response cue** third  

### Boundary failure signs

The embodiment cue has crossed the boundary if:

- it becomes the **emotional center** of the turn  
- it feels **more actionable** than the reflection feels insightful  
- it **pulls attention away** from the reflection  
- the turn starts to feel like “what should I do next?” instead of “what is becoming clear here?”  

### Core rule

If the response cue begins to carry **more force** than the reflection, Milestone F has **drifted**.

---

## Risks or ambiguities

1. **Advice drift** — A cue may start as an invitation but still feel like advice in practice.  
2. **Pressure drift** — A cue may sound gentle on paper but still create subtle self-improvement pressure.  
3. **Generic-response drift** — The system may attach plausible but generic “next responses” that are not truly grounded in the visible pattern.  
4. **Reflection displacement** — Even a short cue can become too central if placement or timing makes it feel like the real point of the turn.  
5. **EN / ZH asymmetry** — Chinese wording may become stiffer or more directive than English wording unless carefully checked.  
6. **Overuse drift** — Even good embodiment cues can become pressuring if surfaced too often. **Silence must remain valid.**
